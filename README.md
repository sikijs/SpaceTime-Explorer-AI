# SpaceTime Explorer AI

## An Interactive Laboratory for Understanding Spacetime and the Universe

SpaceTime Explorer AI is an educational interactive laboratory designed to help
learners understand relativity and its connections to gravity, astronomy,
aerospace, and cosmology.

Instead of simply reading explanations, learners conduct experiments.

The central learning cycle is:

Observe → Predict → Experiment → Analyze → Explain → Mathematics

## Core Subject

Relativity is the foundation of the project.

The project will progressively explore:

- Time and clocks
- Special relativity
- Space and time
- Relativity of simultaneity
- Length contraction
- Spacetime diagrams
- Gravity and gravitational time dilation
- The equivalence principle
- Curved spacetime
- Geodesics
- General relativity
- Black holes
- Gravitational lensing
- Cosmology

## Applications

The principles of relativity will also be explored through:

- Spacecraft
- Orbital mechanics
- Navigation
- GPS
- High-speed missions
- Stars
- Neutron stars
- Black holes
- The expanding universe
- Cosmology

## AI Tutor

An AI tutor will help learners understand the results of experiments.

The physics engine remains authoritative.

The AI interprets the results generated by the simulation and explains them
at an appropriate level for the learner.

## Educational Approach

The project is intended to support several levels of learning:

1. Conceptual understanding
2. Visual exploration
3. Mathematical understanding
4. Advanced derivation

The goal is not to overwhelm the learner with mathematics.

Mathematics will be introduced after the physical idea is understood.

## Development Status

This project is under active development.

The application will be built incrementally, experiment by experiment.

The first development milestone will establish the scientific and educational
design of the first relativity experiment before substantial application
development begins.

## Educational Use

This project is intended for education and independent learning.

It is not intended to replace a university physics course, textbook, or
professional scientific software.
