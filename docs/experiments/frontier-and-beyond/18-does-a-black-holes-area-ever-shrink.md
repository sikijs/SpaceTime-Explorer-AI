## Overview

Experiment 8 of Black Holes showed that a black hole's size depends on its spin: the same mass, spun faster, has a smaller horizon radius. Experiment 10 (Hawking radiation) showed a black hole losing mass very slowly. This experiment asks about **two black holes that merge**: two of about 33.6 and 32.2 Suns came together on 14 January 2025 and made one of about 62.7 Suns. The merger radiated about 3.1 Suns' worth of energy as gravitational waves (Gravitational Waves 1 to 4). Mass went down. **Did the surface of the black hole shrink too?**

In 1971 Stephen Hawking showed that, in general relativity, the total area of black hole horizons **cannot decrease**. GW250114 was the clearest gravitational-wave signal ever recorded (a signal-to-noise ratio of about 80, three to four times the previous best), so the LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA teams could measure the masses and spins before and the mass and spin after well enough to compute the horizon areas, and they found the area after was larger.

The experiment asks three questions:

1. **Which way did the area go, and by how much?** The learner sees the areas before and after, calculated from the published masses and spins.
2. **How much could the merger have radiated before the area law was broken?** The learner sees that radiating about 24 percent (at the measured final spin) would have made the area fall, which the law forbids, so nature stays under a ceiling.
3. **What was actually tested?** The learner sees what was measured (masses and spins from a signal), what was calculated (areas), and what the theorem says.

No new physics beyond one formula: the area of a spinning black hole's horizon, `A = 8π(GM/c²)²(1 + √(1 − χ²))`, where `χ` is the spin (Black Holes 8). The model is **arithmetic on published numbers plus that formula**.

## Learning Objective

By the end the learner can say, in their own words:

1. A black hole's horizon has an area, set by its mass and spin (more mass, a larger area; more spin, a smaller area for the same mass).
2. Hawking's area theorem: in general relativity, the total horizon area never decreases, even when a merger radiates energy and the mass falls.
3. For GW250114 the total area rose from about 240,000 km² (the size of the United Kingdom, in the University of Birmingham's comparison) to about 400,000 km² as reported (about 373,000 km² by the formula), although the mass fell by 4.7 percent.
4. The area law sets a ceiling on how much a merger can radiate (about 24 percent of the mass at the measured final spin, about 29 percent in the simplest case of no spin).
5. What the result is and is not: one very clear event, a statistical statement, a test of a prediction of general relativity.

## Physical Situation

Two black holes of 33.6 and 32.2 Suns (source-frame masses; spins small, at most about 0.25 each) at about 1.14 billion light-years (redshift about 0.09) spiral together and merge into one of about 62.7 Suns with a spin of about 0.68. About 3.1 Suns' worth of energy leaves as gravitational waves. The model treats this as one event with the published numbers and **does not simulate** it.

## Expected Observations

- The total area after is about 1.57 times that before (1.67 in the press figures), although the mass fell by 4.7 percent.
- A merger that radiated more than about 24 percent of the mass would, at the measured final spin, have a smaller area after; the law forbids it.
- A faster final spin means a smaller area for the same mass.
