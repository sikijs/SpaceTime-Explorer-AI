## A difference you should know about (for the owner, not for the learner)
Hawking's 1974 abstract says the temperature is "≈ 10⁻⁶ (M☉/M) K" and the life "of the order of 10⁷¹ (M☉/M)⁻³ s". For the Sun's mass that is about 10⁻⁶ K and 3 × 10⁶³ years. This page uses 6.17 × 10⁻⁸ K and 2.1 × 10⁶⁷ years, from the standard textbook formulas. **Correction (2026-11-19, found by a test while building the physics step):** an earlier version of this draft said the abstract's own formula "works out exactly" to the page's number, so that his figure was a rounded one. That was wrong. Worked literally, the formula as printed in the abstract, (κ/2π)(ħ/2k) with κ = c⁴/4GM, gives half the page's value (about 3.1 × 10⁻⁸ K), and his "≈ 10⁻⁶" is about 16 times the page's value. I have found no page that explains the differences, so the check-up shows Hawking's figure beside ours and says only that they differ and by how much. The page does not say why. The lifetime: his "10⁷¹" is "of the order" and ours is the standard form without the greybody correction. The physics, tests and approved results do not change. *(Open: the page's formulas are still not checked against a textbook or the 1975 paper, S13; Decision 9 of the specification stays open.)*

**NEW: Where this idea comes from.** "In 1974 the physicist Stephen Hawking worked out that quantum physics means a black hole should give off a faint heat, as if it had a temperature. ## 4. Words and formulas (collapsed box)
**Before the first Run:** temperature; kelvin (0 K = −273 °C; each kelvin the size of a Celsius degree); cosmic microwave background (2.725 K, from Cosmology 3); evaporate; Sun (as a mass; 1.99 × 10³⁰ kg); horizon; power of ten (10³ = 1,000, 10⁻³ = 0.001); Hawking radiation; laboratory analogue; primordial black hole; information paradox. **After the first Run:** balance mass; greybody factor ("a real black hole does not glow like a perfect glowing body, so the lifetimes are good only to within a few times"); backreaction (the glow acting back on whatever makes it). **Formulas, with worked examples from the page's own numbers (§15):**
- *Temperature:* T = ħc³ ÷ (8πGMk), which is 6.17 × 10⁻⁸ K ÷ (M in Suns). Worked: for 10 Suns, 6.17 × 10⁻⁸ ÷ 10 = 6.17 × 10⁻⁹ K.
- *Time to evaporate:* t = 5120πG²M³ ÷ (ħc⁴), which is 2.1 × 10⁶⁷ years × (M in Suns)³. Worked: for 10 Suns, 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000, so 2.1 × 10⁷⁰ years.
- *Balance mass:* the mass whose temperature is 2.725 K, about 4.5 × 10²² kg (0.6 of the Moon's mass). Worked: 6.17 × 10⁻⁸ K ÷ 2.725 K = 2.3 × 10⁻⁸, so that is 1 Sun times 2.3 × 10⁻⁸, which is 4.5 × 10²² kg.
(The formulas appear only after the first Run, and the box says, after the Run, that ħ, c, G and k are the Planck constant divided by 2π, the speed of light, the gravitational constant and Boltzmann's constant.)
