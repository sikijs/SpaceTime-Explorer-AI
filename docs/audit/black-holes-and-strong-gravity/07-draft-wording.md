**Things the owner must see.**
1. **The eye's range: 700 or 750?** The specification and the page use 380 to 750 nm ("general knowledge, not checked", Decision 4 and 9). NASA's page (S6) says "typically 380 to 700". With 700 the green lamp would leave the visible range at 7.90 horizon radii (a release at 10) instead of 6.83. The module is not changed by this draft. Options: (a) keep 750 as the page's own convention, said plainly (the page already calls the edges conventions); (b) change to 700 to follow NASA, which changes the module's constant, one number in guess 2's answer text and the tests (a physics change, so yours to decide). Default in this draft: (a), with the wording "about 750 nm (NASA's page says the eye typically detects up to about 700)".
2. **No official page says the wavelength is stretched by the same number as the flashes' spacing.** S1 and S2 say the light is redder or "stretched to longer wavelengths", and S2 calls it gravitational redshift; neither gives the crest-and-flash connection or any number like 870 nm. Those are "calculated here" from the specification's formulas (checked only against each other and against Black Holes 6). A §29 rule 3(c) gap for the status entry.
3. **The real observation is not our model.** S2 is a star orbiting at about 1,400 horizon radii, so its gravitational redshift is tiny (about 0.07 percent, "z ≈ 200 km/s ÷ c"), and it includes a motion effect too. Our traveler falls straight in and is followed down to 0.0001 horizon radii. The block says so. A check I made: gravity alone at 1,400 radii gives 1 ÷ √(1 − 1/1400) − 1 ≈ 0.00036; the paper's combined figure is about 0.00067. These are of the same size, as expected, but I did not derive the paper's number and this check is **not** put on the page.
4. **No ESA page** on gravitational redshift or on the colour of infalling light was found or searched beyond ESO's. **No recent (2025 or 2026) official release** on light from near a horizon was found for this experiment; the page has no "recent news" block and the gap is recorded. 5. **Not read:** the body of the GRAVITY paper, NASA SVS pages, ESA pages. NASA's infrared and microwave pages were read for wavelengths only.
6. **Page dates:** the Goddard page (2015) and the NASA EMS pages (2023, one 2025) are shown in the link lines.
