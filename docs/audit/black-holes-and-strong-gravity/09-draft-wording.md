## Wording changes of 2026-11-18 (all approved by the owner the same day)

*(Made at the owner's request, one passage at a time. They change wording only: no physics, no numbers and no answer are changed, and none gives away what a prediction asks. Where the text above differs, this section is the later and approved wording.)*

1. **Introduction, "The question"** (what "about 3 times too big" means). After "…is only about 3 times too big.": "'Too big' means too big to be a black hole. Any object turns into a black hole if all of its mass is squeezed inside its own critical radius, and that radius is larger for a heavier object. A neutron star holds about 1.4 Suns of mass, so its critical radius is about 4.1 km. Its real radius is 12 km, so it would have to shrink to about a third of its size (12 ÷ 4.1 is about 3). That is a tiny squeeze compared with the Sun, which would have to shrink about 236,000 times. In other words, a neutron star is already almost small enough." (Numbers from Black Holes 1; the 4.1 km and 236,000 are typed in the text.)
2. **Introduction, idea 6 ("What is left depends on what holds the core up")** becomes three bullets, then a spring comparison.
   - **White dwarf:** held up by electrons; stops at about the size of the Earth with about the Sun's mass (Figure 2).
   - **Neutron star:** "Here the core has more mass, and the more mass a core has, the harder its own gravity pulls it in. This pull is too strong for the electrons to hold, so the core keeps shrinking and is squeezed far more tightly. It stops only when neutrons … push back. Shrinking also makes the pull stronger: the same mass packed into a smaller ball pulls harder at its surface, so a smaller core needs a stronger push to hold it up. Why? Gravity gets weaker with distance, and quickly: twice as far, a quarter of the pull; half as far, four times the pull. The surface of a core is one radius away from the center, so when the core shrinks its surface moves closer to all of its mass. Shrink the core to a third of its radius and the pull at its surface is 3 × 3 = 9 times stronger. Compare your own weight: if the Earth were squeezed to half its radius, with the same mass, you would weigh four times as much. A white dwarf is about 6,400 km in radius and a neutron star about 12 km, more than 500 times smaller, so gravity at a neutron star's surface is hundreds of thousands of times stronger than at a white dwarf's of the same mass."
   - **Black hole:** if gravity beats even the neutrons' push, nothing holds the core up, and it shrinks until it is smaller than its horizon radius, the size at which a mass becomes a black hole and not even light can get out (Black Holes 1).
