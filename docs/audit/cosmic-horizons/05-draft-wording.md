## Questions for your decision (§8)
1. **The real MoM-z14 must stay visible.** Today Pictures 1 and 2 follow "Look at another galaxy", so a learner who picks another galaxy loses the real MoM-z14 mark that the guess is about. Proposed: Picture 2's top bar always carries a fixed orange tick for MoM-z14 (new, calculated here) and the white band sits beside it; the marks that follow the control (Picture 1's arrow and line, Picture 2's zoomed-bar marker) are pink. At the start both are MoM-z14. Recommended: yes.
2. **The survey's dot is pink today** (Picture 3, `SURVEY_COLOR` `#f472b6`, and "the pink dot" in the caption). Under the rule pink means the learner's setting. Proposed: recolour the survey's dot and bar to another colour (I would pick teal when building) and reword the caption. Recommended: yes.
3. **Picture 3's scale starts at 1**, so "fewer than the models" cannot be drawn. Proposed: extend the scale to run from 0.1 to 1,000 (one more tick, at 0.1). The module's own edges for the four answers are: fewer below 0.5, same 0.5 to 2, a few times 2 to 50, about a hundred times 50 and up (`countRange`, `earliestGalaxies.ts:99`). I used these instead of the edges I proposed in the report (which left gaps). Recommended: yes.
4. **The count buttons say no numbers.** Proposed: "Fewer than the models predicted (under 0.5 times)", "About the same (0.5 to 2 times)", "A few times more (2 to 50 times)", "About a hundred times more, or higher (50 times or more)", as Prediction 1's buttons already do. This changes approved wording and does not reveal the answer. If you say no, the white band uses the same edges and the caption states them. Recommended: yes.
5. **Open-ended bands.** "About 10 percent (over 4 percent)" and "50 times or more" have no upper edge. Proposed: draw them to the end of their scale with an arrow head. Recommended: yes.

### Captions: Look at / What it means / Why it matters (numbers from the module)
**Picture 1** (stage 1 onward; the old "What to look for" paragraph is removed)
1. *Look at* where the pink line lands, compared with the sky-blue line. *What it means:* the Lyman-alpha light leaves at 121.567 nm, in the ultraviolet, and for MoM-z14 arrives at <1,877> nm, about <1.88> µm, in the infrared (121.567 × <15.44>). *Why it matters:* it is the stretching of space that moved it, and it is why the light of these galaxies cannot be seen with an eye or a telescope for visible light.
2. *Look at* whether the pink line is inside the green bracket. *What it means:* <1.88> µm is inside the range of Webb's NIRCam, <0.6> to <5> µm. *Why it matters:* it is why Webb can find and confirm such galaxies, and why a bigger redshift needs a telescope that reaches further into the infrared.
3. *Look at* the grey dashed arrow. *What it means:* a redshift of 1 stretches the wave 2 times, to <243> nm, still ultraviolet. *Why it matters:* it shows how much bigger the stretch is for a redshift of <14.44>: <15.44> times against 2 times.

**Picture 2** (stage 2 onward)
1. *Look at* the orange tick on the top bar and the white bracket beside it (shown once the tick is drawn). *What it means:* MoM-z14 left <284> million years after the Big Bang, <2.06> percent of the present age, and your guess was <the bracket>. *Why it matters:* it is how early the galaxy already shone: the same share as about <1> month of a child's first four years.
2. *Look at* the orange shading on the zoomed bar and the pink marker. *What it means:* the time that had passed before the light left, for your galaxy (it starts at MoM-z14). *Why it matters:* it lets you see the same galaxy twice, on the whole history and magnified, so you can see how thin the slice is.
3. *Look at* (from stage 3) the three bars. *What it means:* the light travelled <13.51> billion light-years, the galaxy was <2.19> billion light-years away when the light left, and is <33.85> billion light-years away now. *Why it matters:* the three are different because space stretched while the light was on its way, so how far the light travelled is not how far away the galaxy is.
4. *Look at* (from stage 4) the grey-blue dots. *What it means:* the four record holders, from <GN-z11 at 436> to <MoM-z14 at 284> million years, with the last two <7.3> million years apart (JADES-GS-z14-0 at 291). *Why it matters:* each new record moved the earliest confirmed galaxy only a little earlier, which is why it is hard to say how early the earliest galaxies were.

**Picture 3** (stage 5)
1. *Look at* where the survey's dot sits against the violet line at 1. *What it means:* the survey's best estimate is <182> times the models' number, about <2.26> ticks to the right of 1. *Why it matters:* the number of bright galaxies is far above what the models used before Webb predicted.
2. *Look at* the length of the bar and where the dashed 100 line falls in it. *What it means:* the range the paper quotes is <77> to <511> times, and 100 lies inside it. *Why it matters:* the estimate is not precise, so the safest reading is "far more", and a statement that it is 100 times is within the quoted range, not a measured fact.
3. *Look at* the white bracket. *What it means:* where you guessed the number would lie. *Why it matters:* it puts your guess on the same scale as the survey's dot.

**What this table tells us** (drafted, numbers from the module):
1. *The age.* It is a check of the model against the paper. The two ages differ by <4> million years, <1.4> percent. It matters because every time in the pictures comes from this model, so a small gap shows the model is close to the paper's. The experiment has not worked out why they differ.
2. *The count.* The survey's best figure is <182>, the announcements say 100 and the paper's range is <77> to <511>. It matters because the number is far from 1 even at the low end, and it is one survey's estimate with a wide range, so "100 times" is a rounded headline, not a precise number.
3. *The redshift.* Two ways of measuring it give <14.42> and <14.44>, <0.02> apart. It matters because a redshift confirmed two ways is more trustworthy than one from colours alone.
