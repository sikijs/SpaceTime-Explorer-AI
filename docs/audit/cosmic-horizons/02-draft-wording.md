### Captions: Look at / What it means / Why it matters
Each existing "what to look for" list is rewritten into three parts and shown only once its feature is drawn. (Numbers in angle brackets come from the module.)

**Picture 1** (strength of dark energy over time; no mark, always drawn)
1. *Look at* how far the orange line is from the grey one. *What it means:* the grey line is flat at 1×; at z = 1 the orange line is at <1.xx>×. *Why it matters:* if the lines sat on top of each other the description would be a constant dark energy; the gap is how much change this description claims.
2. *Look at* where the orange line is above and where it is below the grey one. *What it means:* above is stronger than today, below is weaker; <the fit crosses at z = ...>. *Why it matters:* this shape is the description's own claim about how dark energy changed, which the later pictures turn into distances.
3. *Look at* whether the three collections give the same line. *What it means:* try the other two; their w₀ and wₐ differ (<values>). *Why it matters:* a fixed fact about the universe might be expected to give one line from all three.
4. The caution stays as a plain note (it is not a "look at").

**Picture 2** (distances and brightness; stage 3 and 4)
1. *Look at* how low the orange line goes. *What it means:* at its lowest it is <-x.xx> %. *Why it matters:* the lower the dip, the easier a survey could notice it; this picture draws the size of the effect, not whether it could be seen.
2. *Look at* where the dip is deepest. *What it means:* <z ≈ x>. *Why it matters:* a survey with many supernovae near that time tells the two universes apart best.
3. *Look at* the far left. *What it means:* the difference there is smaller than in the middle (<-x.xx> % at z = 2). *Why it matters:* a big change in dark energy (Picture 1) did not make a big change in distance; the tutor comes back to the reason.
4. *Look at* the white brackets and the pink dot. *What it means:* the brackets stay at z = 0.5 whatever time you choose; the pink dot moves. *Why it matters:* it separates your guess from the real value at the same time.
Worked moment: kept, with the learner's numbers (100 units becomes <99.xx> units; brightness <x.xx> %).

**Picture 3** (sigma bars; stage 5 and 6)
1. *Look at* whether any bar reaches the line at 5. *What it means:* the longest bar is <4.2>; none reaches 5. *Why it matters:* 5 is the usual bar for a discovery, so no collection alone meets it.
2. *Look at* whether the bars agree. *What it means:* from <2.8> to <4.2> (the three fits). *Why it matters:* the same galaxies and microwave background went into each, only the supernovae changed.
3. *Look at* the two DES bars and the white arrow. *What it means:* DES Y5 is <4.2> and the recalibrated bar <3.2>; the arrow starts at 4.2. *Why it matters:* the same supernovae, calibrated differently, give different numbers, so a calibration step can move the result.
4. *Look at* the last row (stage 6). *What it means:* <2.7> sigma for the DESI and microwave background combination with the July 2026 analysis. *Why it matters:* a newer result need not point the same way as an older one.

Note, "What this table tells us": (1) *w today.* A constant dark energy has w = −1 exactly. <w₀> is <x.xxx> above it, so this description says dark energy is slightly different from constant today. It matters because every other row grows from this and wₐ. (2) *Density.* A constant dark energy keeps 1× at every time; <x.xxx>× at z = <z> means <stronger/weaker> than today by <x> percent. It matters because it is the cause that Picture 2 turns into distances. (3) *Distance.* 0 % would be no change; <−x.xx> % means the galaxy looks nearer. It matters because it is the signal that surveys look for. (4) *Sigma.* 5 is the usual bar; <x.x> is <less>. It matters because it says how strong the hint is, not how likely it is that dark energy really changes.
