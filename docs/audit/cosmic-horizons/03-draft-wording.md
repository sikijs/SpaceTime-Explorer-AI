### Captions: Look at / What it means / Why it matters (numbers from the module)
**The weak-lensing illustration** (above Part 1; wording unchanged except the look-for list)
1. *Look at* the jump from panel B to panel C. *What it means:* in B the lean is hidden in the crowd of 250 galaxies' shapes; in C, averaging many galaxies makes it clear. *Why it matters:* this averaging is the whole trick of weak lensing.
2. *Look at* the orange lines going round the bump. *What it means:* they run round the bump like the rungs of a ring. *Why it matters:* a pattern going round a spot says there is matter there, though nothing is visible.
3. *Look at* the grey lines. *What it means:* they are almost points. *Why it matters:* with no matter in the way, the average has no direction, so a lean that does show up can be trusted as a signal.

**Picture 1** (stage 3 onward; the "white brackets" in the existing caption become "pale brackets", because white now means only a guess)

Readings come from the module as "more", "less" or "the same", with no invented cut-off; the DES row says only that the comparison is not like-for-like. The check-up always uses all four, whichever survey you chose.
Note, "What this table tells us": (1) *Planck's S₈.* The formula gives 0.831 from Planck's own σ₈ and Ωm; the page uses the quoted 0.834. It matters because every sigma on this page is measured from that value. The page does not say why the two differ by 0.003. (2) *KiDS-1000.* Worked out here: 2.60, a little less than the paper's "about 3"; the papers use fuller statistics. It matters because it is the first tension the experiment is about, so read this page's number as an approximation. (3) *KiDS-Legacy.* 0.84 here, a little more than the paper's 0.73, for the same reason. (4) *DES.* The paper's 2.0 and 2.3 compare DES with Planck + ACT + SPT (a different baseline), and this page's 1.69 and 2.05 compare it with Planck alone, so they are not the same comparison and the page gives no reading. It matters because the numbers look close but answer different questions.
