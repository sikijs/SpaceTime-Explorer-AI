### Captions: Look at / What it means / Why it matters (numbers from the module)
**Picture 1** (stage 2 onward; the old sentence "What to look for after Run" is removed)
1. *Look at* where the pink marker sits, to the left or right of the sky-blue release marker. *What it means:* for your total (it starts at DESI's limit, <0.0592> eV) it is to the right: z = <36.4>, about <74> million years after the Big Bang, against about 380,000 years for the release. *Why it matters:* it says whether the neutrinos were still light-like or already matter-like when the light of the microwave background was set free.
2. *Look at* how far apart the two markers are. *What it means:* about <190> times later. *Why it matters:* the neutrinos acted like light for a long time, which is why the microwave background and later pictures see them differently.
3. *Look at* the grey circle (one neutrino carrying the whole total). *What it means:* it turns heavy at z = <109>, much earlier; the split of the total changes the time, but not which side of the release it falls on, for these totals. *Why it matters:* the answer to "before or after" does not depend on how the mass is shared. (Shown only once the grey circle is drawn.)
4. *Look at* the white marker. *What it means:* it stays where you guessed, whatever total you choose. *Why it matters:* it keeps your guess for 0.06 eV apart from the pink marks for your own total.

**Picture 2** (stage 3 and 4)
1. *Look at* how thin the neutrinos' sliver is. *What it means:* <0.44> percent of the matter at DESI's limit. *Why it matters:* a small share can still change the smallest clumps, which is the effect that surveys of the sky can measure.
2. *Look at* where the yellow dot sits on the smoothing line. *What it means:* <3.5> percent smoothing at DESI's limit, about 8 times the share. *Why it matters:* it is the size of the effect that gives the sky a way to weigh the neutrinos.
3. *Look at* what a larger total does (try it in Part 2). *What it means:* at 0.5 eV the share is <3.75> percent and the smoothing <30> percent. *Why it matters:* a larger total gives a larger share and a larger effect, so a heavier total would be easier to see, and a limit on the effect is a limit on the total.

**Picture 3** (stage 5)
1. *Look at* how far each orange bar reaches to the right of the first green line. *What it means:* the March 2025 ceiling is <9> percent above it (<1.092> times); the July 2026 ceiling is <0.7> percent above it (<1.007> times). *Why it matters:* it is the room left for the normal ordering, and it has nearly closed.
2. *Look at* how that distance changed from the top bar to the bottom bar. *What it means:* the bars shorten from Planck's <0.12> to DESI's <0.0592>. *Why it matters:* the limits from the sky have come down toward the floor that the laboratory sets.
3. *Look at* where each orange bar ends compared with the second green line. *What it means:* the 2025 and 2026 ceilings end before it, <0.0642> and <0.0592> against <0.1004>. *Why it matters:* if the inverted ordering were true, the total would have to be at least <0.1004> eV, so these ceilings leave it no room.
4. *Look at* the white band. *What it means:* where you guessed the ceiling lies. *Why it matters:* it puts your guess on the same scale as the real bars.

It matters because it was the loose limit before DESI. The floors come from two mass gaps whose numbers were taken from a search result, not read at the paper's table, so treat them as approximate.
