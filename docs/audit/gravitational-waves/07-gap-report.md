The Hubble constant, 3,017 ÷ 40 = 75.4 km/s/Mpc, is then the recession velocity divided by the distance the page chose. This is a consistency check on the formula, not a measurement. In the real method nature chooses the distance and the detector returns only the detected amplitude. The page needs to say so (question 5).
- **New finding O14, the published Hubble-constant figures are not on the page and one is inconsistent.** The page gives the real range as "62 to 170 km/s/Mpc (about ±15% around the central estimate)" (:68), but 62 to 170 is not 15 percent around any one centre. A check-up against the published result needs those numbers read from the paper, which Step 2 does (arXiv 1710.05835); none is assumed here.
- **New finding O15, "standard candle" is named without a plain meaning.** The introduction defines "standard siren" as the gravitational-wave version of "using a light source of known brightness", which is the idea of a standard candle, but never says "standard candle" or what a candle is. Step 2 gives it a plain definition at the point where it is needed.
