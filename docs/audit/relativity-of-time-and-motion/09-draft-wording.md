## 3. Words and formulas (new box, collapsed, above Part 1)

- **The same moment / simultaneous:** two things happening at the same time. **Event:** something that happens at one place and one moment, one point on the diagram. **Worldline:** the line that shows where something was at every moment. **Position:** where something is along the line the block moves on, in light-seconds. **Time:** when, in seconds, measured from the moment the flash was released.
- **Lab:** the place where the experiment is set up, where the measuring is done. **Frame (reference frame):** the point of view of someone standing still in their own group, measuring with their own clocks and rulers. The lab is one; the moving block has its own.
- **Block:** a long, flat block, like a train car lying on the ground, half a light-second long when it stands still. **Back end / front end:** the end the block moves away from, and the end it moves towards. **Flash:** a short burst of light released from the exact middle of the block.
- **Light-second:** the distance light travels in one second, about 300,000 km. **c:** the speed of light, about 300,000 km every second; "0.5c" is half of that.
- **The lab's "same moment" line:** a level line on the diagram, because every point on it is at the same time. **The block's own "same moment" line:** the line that joins the events that someone riding with the block calls simultaneous. **Lean:** how much a line tilts, found as the time it rises divided by the position it goes across.
- **Formula, lean = time ÷ position.** Example that uses no moving block: a line that rises 0.1 s while it goes 0.4 light-second across leans 0.1 ÷ 0.4 = 0.25 s for every light-second. A level line rises 0 s, so it leans 0. *(The lean of the block's own line is withheld until after the run, because it is the answer to the guess.)*

## 6. Part 1's diagram

**6a. The sketch "What a level line means"** *(in the introduction, after "A line for 'the same moment'")*. Title **"What a level line means (a sketch, not the real diagram)"**. Two pictures at one scale: **"A LEVEL LINE"**: two dots at positions −0.2 and +0.2 light-second, both at time 0.3 s, joined by a level line, with "same height: the same moment" written beside it; **"A LEANING LINE"**: two dots at positions −0.2 and +0.2, at times 0.2 s and 0.3 s, joined by a leaning line, with "different heights: different moments" beside it. Position and time arrows on each, ticks every 0.1.

*Caption, "How to read this sketch":*
- **What it is.** A small picture to help you read the real diagram further down. It has no block and no flash in it, and it does not show any result of the experiment. In both pictures, **position** runs across in light-seconds and **time** runs up in seconds.
- **Each dot** is an event: something that happens at one place and one moment. The height of a dot is the time it happens, and how far across it is shows where.
- **The line** joins the two dots. **On the left** the dots are at the same height, so the line is **level**. **On the right** the dots are at different heights, so the line **leans**.
- **A worked moment.** On the left both dots are at 0.3 s, so the line rises 0.3 − 0.3 = 0 s over 0.4 light-second across: it leans 0 ÷ 0.4 = 0. On the right the dots are at 0.2 s and 0.3 s, so the line rises 0.1 s over the same 0.4 light-second: it leans 0.1 ÷ 0.4 = 0.25 s for every light-second.
- *What to look for:* (1) **Look at** the two dots on the left and the level line between them. **What it means:** both happen at 0.3 s, at the same moment, according to the lab. **Why it matters:** any level line on a diagram joins events that are at the same moment for the lab. (2) **Look at** the two dots on the right and the leaning line between them. **What it means:** one is at 0.2 s and the other at 0.3 s, 0.1 s apart, so the lab says they happen at different moments. **Why it matters:** a line that leans joins events at different moments, according to the lab. The lean, 0.25, says how much the time changes for each light-second across.
- **Where this picture comes from.** *Imagined for illustration:* the dots and their times. *Measured:* nothing. *Calculated here:* only the positions drawn, from the times and positions above.

Nothing on the diagram is a 'what if'. The white marker is only your guess."

**Caption, "How to read this diagram"** (appears when the drawing starts; no times, no answers):
- **The picture.** A spacetime diagram of one flash and one block moving at 0.5c: the one from "Drawing Spacetime". **Position** runs across, in light-seconds (a light-second is the distance light travels in one second, about 300,000 km). **Time** runs up, in seconds, from the moment the flash was released. Both axes use the same scale, so no line's lean is stretched.
- **The amber dot** is the flash being released: position 0, time 0.
- **The two solid lines** are the block's two ends: **blue** is the back end, **purple** is the front end. **The two dashed lines** are the flash going towards each end, in the same colours. **The dots** appear where a dashed line meets the solid line of the same colour. Each is an **event**: the flash reaching that end. The number beside it is the time, in seconds.
- **After the dots, two lines appear.** Each one is a "same moment" line: a line that says "every point on me happens at the same moment." They belong to two different people, so they are drawn in two different colours.
- **The green line** is the new one. It is drawn straight through the two dots. It belongs to the person riding on the block. Remember, that person says the flash reached the back end and the front end at the same moment. The two dots are exactly those two arrivals, so the green line is the line that joins them. It is the block's own "same moment" line.
- **Why a "same moment" line can have two different heights.** The height up the side of the diagram is the time on the *lab's* clocks. The person riding on the block uses their own clocks, not the lab's. By the rider's clocks the flash reached both ends at the same moment, even though the lab's clocks show two different times for those same two arrivals. So a line joining events that are "the same moment" for the rider does not have to be level on a diagram that is drawn with the lab's time going up.
- **In short:** for the lab, the flash reaches the back end and the front end at two different times. For the rider on the block, it reaches both ends at the same time. Neither is a mistake: both people are measuring the same two events, each with their own clocks.
- **The green line is drawn by the lab's clock.** Every height on this diagram, including each end of the green line, is read from the lab's clock. The times written beside the two dots are the lab's clock readings. The green line only joins the two dots; it does not show the rider's own clock readings, which are not drawn here.
- **The pale grey dashed line** belongs to the lab, the person standing still and watching. For the lab, a "same moment" line is a level line, the kind that does not lean up or down. It is drawn through the lower dot. It is there for one reason: so you can hold the block's line up against the lab's line and compare them.
- **The white marker** (when the green line appears) is your guess. Its lean is only an example, not the real lean. It is only a marker.
- **How it is drawn.** The lines grow upward over a few seconds. Playback speed only changes how long the drawing takes, not what is drawn.
- **When the drawing ends:** the green and grey lines appear, and the worked moment and "What to look for" appear below.

*A worked moment, step by step (at 0.5c; appears when the drawing ends).* (1) The flash reaches the back end at **0.144 s**, at position **−0.144** light-second, and the front end at **0.433 s**, at position **0.433** (the blue and purple dots, as in "Drawing Spacetime"). (2) The green line passes through both dots. Going from the blue dot to the purple dot it rises 0.433 − 0.144 = **0.289 s** while it goes 0.433 − (−0.144) = **0.577 light-second** across. (3) Its lean is 0.289 ÷ 0.577 = **0.500 s for every light-second**. (4) A level line would lean 0 (it rises 0 s). The green line leans 0.500, so it is not level. *(At 0c both dots are at the same height, so the line rises 0 s and leans 0.)*

*What to look for* (each: **Look at** / **What it means** / **Why it matters**; an item appears only once its feature is drawn):
1. **Look at** the green line and the grey dashed line. **What it means:** the green line leans (0.500 s for every light-second) and the grey line is level (0). **Why it matters:** the block's own "same moment" line is a different line from the lab's.
2. **Look at** the two dots and the green line through them. **What it means:** the green line passes through both events, the same two you already saw. **Why it matters:** nothing new was calculated. The line was already there in the two events; you only drew it.
3. **Look at** the white marker and the green line. **What it means:** the marker shows the choice you made, and its lean is only an example. **Why it matters:** it puts what you expected beside what was drawn.
