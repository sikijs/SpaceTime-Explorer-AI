## 3. "Words and formulas" (collapsed box)

- **Clock:** anything that repeats in a very regular way and keeps count of the repeats.
- **Event:** one moment at which something happens: here, the start of the experiment and its end.
- **Clock reading:** the numbers a clock shows at an event, written hours:minutes:seconds. 00:00:10 means 0 hours, 0 minutes, 10 seconds.
- **Elapsed time:** how much time passed between two events, according to a clock.
- **Duration:** how long the experiment is set to last, in seconds.
- **At rest:** not moving compared with the other things in the picture.
- **Synchronized:** set to show the same reading at the same moment. Both clocks here start synchronized, at 00:00:00.
- **Reference frame:** a group of people and clocks that are all standing still relative to one another, so that none moves compared with the others.
- **Formulas (shown only after the Part 1 run, because they use its answer, §12):** elapsed time = clock reading at the end − clock reading at the start; difference = the larger elapsed time − the smaller. Worked example from this page: Clock A: 00:00:10 − 00:00:00 = 10 seconds; Clock B: 00:00:10 − 00:00:00 = 10 seconds; difference = 10 − 10 = 0 seconds.

## 6. Part 1's pictures: the two clocks and the two-row Event Interval strip

**Drawn:** the two clock readouts side by side ("Clock A", "Clock B"), each counting up. When the run ends, the strip: two rows on one shared scale from 0 to 20 seconds (twice the duration) with ticks and numbers at 0, 10 and 20 s. Row "Clock A" on top, row "Clock B" below. On each row an amber dot at 0 s (**Event A, START**, reading 00:00:00) and an amber dot at 10 s (**Event B, END**, reading 00:00:10), and the words "elapsed: 10 seconds" between them. A thin dashed amber line joins the two rows at each event, so the learner sees that both clocks are read at the same two moments. Under the lower row, the labels "Event A, START" and "Event B, END". **The white marks:** on each row a white dot at the guessed number of seconds, joined by a line to a white box "YOUR GUESS for Clock A: N seconds" (above row A) and "YOUR GUESS for Clock B: M seconds" (between the rows). A guess beyond the scale (or below 0) is a white arrow at the edge and its box still says the number and "(off the scale)". Each mark appears only when the strip appears (§12).

**"Where the real picture comes from":** "Calculated here, not measured: the readings come from the length of the experiment, one second of clock time for each second that passes, for each clock. No instrument measured them and nothing here is imagined. The white marks are only your guesses, placed on the same scale."

**Caption, "How to read the Event Interval strip":**
*What you see.*
- **The two clocks** above show each clock's reading as hours:minutes:seconds (00:00:10 is 10 seconds). Both count up while the experiment runs and stay at their last reading when it ends.
- **The strip** appears when the run ends. It is a time line: the further right, the later the moment. It has one row for each clock, and both rows share one scale in seconds. 0 s is the start, 10 s the length of the experiment and 20 s twice that, so there is room to the right of the end.
- **Event A, START** is the moment the experiment began, and **Event B, END** the moment it ended. The dashed amber lines join the two rows at each event, so you can see that both clocks are read at the same two moments. The number under each dot is that clock's reading at that event.
- **"Elapsed"** is the time that passed between the two events on that clock.
- **The white marks and the YOUR GUESS boxes** are your guesses, one per clock, placed on the same scale. A white arrow at the edge means your guess is beyond the end of the scale. A mark is only your guess; it never moves the events.

*A worked moment.* The experiment lasts 10 seconds. On Clock A, Event A is at 0 s and reads 00:00:00, and Event B is at 10 s and reads 00:00:10, so its elapsed time is 00:00:10 − 00:00:00 = 10 seconds. Clock B's row is read the same way. If you had guessed 7 for Clock A, its white mark would sit at 7 s, 3 seconds to the left of Event B.

*What to look for.* (Each item appears only once what it refers to is drawn.)
1. **Look at** the two amber dots at Event B, one on each row, and the dashed line that joins them. **What it means:** it is the same moment on both clocks, and the readings under the dots are the readings of Clock A and Clock B at that moment. **Why it matters:** the question of this experiment is whether two clocks in different places show the same reading at the same moment.
2. **Look at** the two "elapsed" labels. **What it means:** each clock's own elapsed time, from its own start reading to its own end reading, here both 10 seconds. **Why it matters:** it shows how much time each clock says passed, which can be compared directly.
3. **Look at** the white YOUR GUESS marks and the Event B dot on the same row. **What it means:** the gap between a mark and the dot (if any) is how far that guess was from that clock's end reading, in seconds. **Why it matters:** it puts what you expected for each clock beside what the clock showed.

## 10. Physics module (new, with tests; the approved model is unchanged)

In `twoClockExperiment.ts`:
- `clockReadingsAt(durationSeconds, simulationTime)` returns Clock A's and Clock B's readings at a simulation time between 0 and the duration (each clock read separately from the model, both equal; the UI floors them for display).
- `twoClockStripScaleEnd(durationSeconds)` returns twice the duration.
- `guessPositionsOnStrip(durationSeconds, guessA, guessB)` returns, for each clock, the scale's end, the guess's position as a fraction 0 to 1 (clamped), which edge it is off (`'left'`, `'right'` or none) and the guess minus that clock's real elapsed time. It refuses a guess that is not finite.

Tests (in `twoClockExperiment.test.ts`):
1. At time 0 both readings are 0; at time = the duration both equal the duration; halfway both equal half.
2. The two clocks' readings are equal at every sampled time, for 5, 10, 20, 30 and 60 seconds.
3. The scale ends at twice the duration.
4. A guess equal to the duration sits at 0.5, 0 at 0, twice the duration at 1, for each clock separately (a guess of 7 for A and 12 for B gives 0.35 and 0.6).
5. A guess beyond the scale is clamped to 1 and flagged `'right'`; a negative guess is clamped to 0 and flagged `'left'`.
6. Each difference is that guess minus that clock's real elapsed time (A 7 against 10 gives −3; B 12 gives +2).
7. The real result does not depend on the guesses.
8. A non-finite guess is refused.
