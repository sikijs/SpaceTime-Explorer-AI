## 3. Words and formulas (new box, collapsed, above Part 1)

- **Spacetime diagram:** a graph with position across and time up. **Position:** where something is along the line the block moves on, in light-seconds. **Time:** when, in seconds, measured from the moment the flash was released. **Event:** something that happens at one place and one moment, one point on the diagram. **Worldline:** the line that shows where something was at every moment. **Lean:** how far a line tilts sideways as it goes up; a faster object leans more.
- **Block:** a long, flat block, like a train car lying on the ground, half a light-second long when it stands still. **Rest length:** its length when it stands still. **Length seen from the lab:** its length as measured by the lab when it moves along its own length (shorter than the rest length, as found in "Does motion change length?"). **Back end / front end:** the end the block moves away from, and the end it moves towards. **Lab:** the place where the experiment is set up, where the measuring is done.
- **Flash:** a short burst of light released from the exact middle of the block. **Light-second:** the distance light travels in one second, about 300,000 km. **c:** the speed of light, about 300,000 km every second; "0.5c" is half of that. **At rest / moving:** standing still compared with the lab, or not.
- **Formula, time = distance ÷ speed.** Example from the page's numbers: light goes at c, which is 1 light-second every second, so to cover 0.2 light-second it needs 0.2 ÷ 1 = 0.2 second. That is why the light's line in the second sketch leans 0.2 light-second for every 0.2 s it rises. *(The sums for the moving block are withheld: they would give the guess away.)*

## 6. Part 1's diagram

**The diagram** (same drawing as now, on a **dark ground**, with labels added on the canvas): the title inside the picture, "SPACETIME DIAGRAM: THE BLOCK MOVING AT 0.5c"; a position axis along the bottom with an arrow and the name "position (light-seconds) →" and tick marks and numbers every 0.1; a time axis up the left with an arrow and "time (seconds) ↑" and tick marks and numbers every 0.1; the amber dot at the origin labelled "Flash released here"; the blue solid line labelled "Back end of the block" and the purple solid line "Front end of the block"; the blue dashed line "Light going to the back end" and the purple dashed line "Light going to the front end"; when the lines reach each other, a dot with "Back end reached: 0.144 s" or "Front end reached: 0.433 s". **The white guess marker** (appears when the dots do): for "the back end's worldline first", a white ring round the back-end dot; for "the front end's worldline first", a white ring round the front-end dot; for "both at the same time", a white bracket joining the two dots. The marker has no size or position of its own beyond pointing at its target, and no white thing moves a dot or a line.

**"Where the real picture comes from"** (under the diagram): "**Measured:** nothing on this page. **Calculated here:** every line and dot, from the speed of light, c, and the block's length seen from the lab, using the rules of "Why can't light go faster?" and "Does motion change length?", and the times of "At the same time... for whom?". **Imagined for the question:** the block and the flash are an imagined scene, a thought experiment that nobody has built. Nothing on the diagram is a 'what if'. The white marker is only your guess."

**Caption, "How to read this diagram"** (appears when the drawing starts; no times, no answers):
- **The picture.** A spacetime diagram of one flash and one block. **Position** runs across, in light-seconds (a light-second is the distance light travels in one second, about 300,000 km). **Time** runs up, in seconds, measured from the moment the flash was released. Both axes use the same scale, so a line's lean is not stretched.
- **The amber dot** is the flash being released: position 0, time 0.
- **The two solid lines** are the block's two ends: **blue** is the back end, **purple** is the front end. They lean over together because the block is moving, and they stay the same distance apart because the block does not stretch.
- **The two dashed lines** are the flash: **blue** is the part going towards the back end and **purple** the part going towards the front end. They start at the amber dot and form a V. Light's lean is the same at every block speed.
- **The dots** appear where a dashed line meets a solid line of the same colour. Each is an **event**: the flash reaching that end. The number beside it is the time, in seconds, at which it happened.
- **The white marker** (once the dots appear) is your guess. It points at the choice you made. It is only a marker.
- **How it is drawn.** The lines grow upward over a few seconds. Playback speed only changes how long the drawing takes, not what is drawn.
- **When the drawing ends:** the lines stop at the later of the two events, and the worked moment and "What to look for" appear below.

*A worked moment, step by step (at 0.5c; appears when the drawing ends).* (1) The block is 0.500 light-seconds long at rest. As seen from the lab it is shorter, **0.433 light-seconds**, so each end starts 0.2165 light-second from the middle, where the flash is released. (2) **The back end** runs towards the flash and the flash runs towards it, so the gap between them closes at c + 0.5c = 1.5c: 0.2165 ÷ 1.5 = **0.144 s**. In that time the back end has moved to position −0.2165 + 0.5 × 0.144 = **−0.144**. That is the blue dot. (3) **The front end** runs away from the flash, so the flash gains on it at c − 0.5c = 0.5c: 0.2165 ÷ 0.5 = **0.433 s**. The front end has then moved to 0.2165 + 0.5 × 0.433 = **0.433**. That is the purple dot. (4) The blue dot is 0.289 s lower than the purple dot. Lower on the diagram means earlier.

*What to look for* (each: **Look at** / **What it means** / **Why it matters**; an item appears only once its feature is drawn):
1. **Look at** the blue dot and the purple dot. **What it means:** the flash reached the back end at 0.144 s and the front end at 0.433 s, and the blue dot sits lower. **Why it matters:** this is the answer to your guess, read straight off the diagram.
2. **Look at** the height between the two dots. **What it means:** it is 0.289 s: the time between the two events. **Why it matters:** it turns "at the same time or not" into a distance you can see: 0 would mean the dots are level.
3. **Look at** the two solid lines and the dashed line each one meets. **What it means:** the back end leans towards the light's path, and the front end leans away from it. **Why it matters:** this is what makes the two meetings happen at different heights. The tutor will help you work out why.
4. **Look at** the white marker and the dot it points at. **What it means:** it shows the choice you made. **Why it matters:** it puts what you expected beside what was drawn.
