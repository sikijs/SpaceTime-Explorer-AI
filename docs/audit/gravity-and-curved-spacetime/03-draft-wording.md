## 3. "Words and formulas" (collapsed box)

- **Sealed cabin:** a closed box with no windows, so the only thing you can watch is what happens inside it.
- **Acceleration:** how fast something's speed is changing. A car that goes from 0 to 10 m/s in 2 seconds is accelerating at 5 metres per second, every second.
- **g:** the acceleration of a dropped object near Earth's ground, 9.8 m/s². 2g is twice that and 0.5g half.
- **Separation:** the distance between the two balls, measured side to side, in metres. It starts at 1 m.
- **Left ball / right ball:** the two identical balls, one on each side of the cabin. They are drawn in teal (left) and red (right) so you can follow each one.
- **Strength:** a plain number (no unit) that stands in for how big the cabin is compared with the planet. Real planets are so much bigger than cabins that the real number is far too small to see, so the values here are exaggerated.
- **First-order:** a simplified version of the real physics that keeps the biggest effect and ignores tiny corrections.
- **Graph (line):** a picture of how one number changes as another does. Here each line shows the separation at each moment.
- **Tidal effect:** the name for what the tutor explains after your run.
- **Formula (the one this page uses before the run):** time to reach the floor = √(2 × height ÷ acceleration). **Worked example from this page:** at 1g (9.8 m/s²) from 2 m: √(2 × 2 ÷ 9.8) = √0.408 = 0.64 seconds. At 2g: √(2 × 2 ÷ 19.6) = 0.45 seconds.

*The formula for how the separation changes with time is **not** in this box: it would show the answer before the learner has predicted (§12). It appears in the Part 1 caption after the run.*

**Caption, "How to read this diagram":**
*What you see.*
- **The two cabins** show the same moment in two different situations. Each has two balls (teal on the left, red on the right) 2 metres above the floor at the start, 1 metre apart. The number under each cabin is the distance between its balls right now.
- **The green arrow in the planet cabin** shows gravity pulling the balls down. **The red arrow in the rocket cabin** shows the rocket pushing the cabin, floor included, up. The rocket does not touch the balls. A longer arrow means a bigger g.
- **The graph** draws itself as the balls fall. Up the side is the distance between the balls, from 0 to 1.2 m (the dotted line is the 1 m start). Along the bottom is the time since release, to the moment the balls reach the floor.
- **The solid indigo line** is the planet cabin. **The dashed amber line** is the rocket cabin. If the two lines lay on top of each other, you would see the amber dashes along the indigo line.
- **The two white rings, arrows and YOUR GUESS boxes** are your two guesses, drawn once both lines are drawn. A ring is in the same place whatever you guessed. Its arrow shows only the direction you guessed (down: closer together; flat bar: the same distance; up: farther apart), and its length means nothing. Both are markers only, and neither moves a line.
- **The formulas used:** separation = 1 m × (1 − strength × (time ÷ fall time)²) for the planet cabin, and 1 m at every moment for the rocket cabin. Height = 2 m − ½ × acceleration × time².

*A worked moment.* At 1g the balls need 0.64 seconds to fall 2 metres. Halfway through the fall, at 0.32 seconds, the planet cabin's separation is 1 × (1 − 0.3 × 0.5²) = 1 × (1 − 0.075) = 0.925 m. At the end, 1 × (1 − 0.3 × 1²) = 0.700 m. The rocket cabin's is 1.000 m at both moments.

*What to look for.* (Each item appears only once what it refers to is drawn.)
1. **Look at** the solid indigo line and the dashed amber line. **What it means:** each shows the distance between one cabin's two balls at every moment. **Why it matters:** it lets you compare the two cabins and see whether the distance changes, instead of only reading the number at the end.
2. **Look at** the right end of each line. **What it means:** it shows the distance between the balls when they reach the floor: 0.700 m for the planet cabin and 1.000 m for the rocket cabin in this run, against the 1 m start. **Why it matters:** it gives you a number for each cabin to compare with the start.
3. **Look at** the two white rings and your two YOUR GUESS boxes. **What it means:** each marks the place on the graph where the answer to that guess can be read: the end of its line. **Why it matters:** it shows where to look to compare what you expected with what was drawn.
