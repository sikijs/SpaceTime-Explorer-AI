## 3. "Words and formulas" (collapsed box)

- **Tick:** one beat of a clock, like one "tock" of a ticking clock. The more beats a clock counts in the same stretch of time, the faster it is running.
- **Floor clock / ceiling clock:** the two identical clocks fixed to the floor and to the ceiling of the rocket cabin. The floor clock is the yardstick: it counts one tick per second of its own time.
- **Rocket cabin:** a closed box with a rocket pushing it, so its speed keeps growing, as in Experiment 1.
- **Strength:** a stand-in number for two real things combined: how hard the rocket accelerates and how far apart the clocks are. A bigger strength means a bigger difference between the clocks. It is exaggerated here so you can see the effect. A real rocket or building would have a strength far too small to see.
- **Frequency ratio:** the fraction 1 − strength. It tells you how many ticks the floor clock counts for each tick the ceiling clock counts. At strength 0.3 the ratio is 0.70: for every tick at the ceiling, the floor counts 0.70.
- **First-order:** a simplified version of the real physics that keeps the biggest effect and ignores tiny corrections. This experiment uses it, not the full exact treatment.
- **Redshift:** a wave arriving stretched out, spread over a little more time than it was sent in. The name comes from visible light, where a stretched light wave shifts toward red. The tutor explains it after your run.
- **Gravitational time dilation:** the name for what the tutor explains after your run.
- **Graph (curve, line):** a picture of how one number changes as another does. Here each line shows one clock's tick count at each moment.
- **Formulas (the ones this page uses).** Floor ticks = time on the floor clock, in seconds. Ceiling ticks = floor ticks ÷ (1 − strength). **Worked example from this page:** at strength 0.3, after 10 seconds the floor clock has counted 10 ticks, and the ceiling clock 10 ÷ (1 − 0.3) = 10 ÷ 0.70 = 14.29 ticks. At strength 0.6: 10 ÷ 0.40 = 25.00 ticks.

(Checked: 10/0.7 = 14.2857; 10/0.4 = 25. Both agree with `tickCountAt`.)
